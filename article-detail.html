Digital Tax Administration, Compliance Costs, and Revenue Mobilization in Emerging African Economies

Prof. Nasiru Musa Yauri1*, Associate Prof. Sani Damamisau Mohammed2
1 Faculty of Management Sciences, Federal University Dutse / Al-Qalam University Katsina, Nigeria
2 Department of Taxation, Faculty of Management Sciences, Federal University Dutse, Jigawa State, Nigeria
* Corresponding Author: ajftfp@fud.edu.ng
Received: Dec 12, 2025
Revised: Jan 10, 2026
Accepted: Jan 22, 2026
Published: Jan 31, 2026
Journal: African Journal of Finance, Taxation, and Fiscal Policy (AJFTFP), Vol. 1, No. 1, pp. 12–29
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/ajftfp.2026.0101

Abstract

Research Problem: The proliferation of digital tax administration platforms across emerging African economies is designed to optimize non-oil revenue mobilization, reduce tax leakages, and expand the formal fiscal net. However, empirical questions remain regarding the unintended compliance costs imposed on small and micro-enterprises (SMEs) and the real net elasticity of digital revenue collections.

Methodology: This study employs an unbalanced panel dataset of 12 Sub-Saharan African economies from 2014 to 2024 using a Generalized Method of Moments (System-GMM) econometric framework paired with structured enterprise-level surveys in Nigeria.

Key Findings: Empirical results reveal that a 10% increase in digital tax platform integration leads to an average 2.48% increase in direct tax revenue mobilization, primarily driven by automated withholding and electronic filing verification. Nevertheless, compliance costs for informal micro-enterprises rose by 8.4% during initial adoption phases before declining following simplified mobile-USSD payment rollouts.

Contribution to Knowledge: The study provides an actionable policy framework balancing digital fiscal enforcement with simplified taxpayer interfaces, recommending tiered threshold exemptions and subsidized digital infrastructure for micro-traders in African markets.
