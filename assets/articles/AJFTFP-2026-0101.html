Audit Quality, Corporate Investments Structure, and Corporate Tax Avoidance: Evidence from Nigerian Financial Service Sector

ABSTRACT              Published Online: 06/24/2026

Nigeria as a country cannot generate sufficient revenue from taxation because it faces institutional and structural challenges in revenue generation. This is true owing to the fact that Nigeria's tax system is marked by numerous taxes and revenue collection agencies, a disjointed and intricate structure, low tax compliance, widespread tax evasion, complex costs of revenue administration, and inadequate coordination between fiscal and economic policies. Therefore, this study examines how audit quality and investment structure jointly influence corporate tax avoidance among listed financial service firms in the NXG. The study employs a balanced panel dataset over the period 2015 to 2021. Tax avoidance is proxied by book-tax differences (BTD), while audit quality is measured using audit size and audit tenure. Investment structure is proxied by short-term debt securities and PPE. The study adopts fixed-effects estimation with lagged explanatory variables and robust standard errors. The results shows that audit tenure exert a negative and statistically insignificant effect on tax avoidance, suggesting that prolonged auditor-client relationships do not necessarily impair audit independence. Investment in short-term debt securities exhibits a positive and significant effect on tax avoidance, while PPE investment shows a significant negative relationship with tax avoidance. The findings support agency theory and tax planning theory, highlighting the role of investment structure in shaping corporate tax behavior. The study suggests that future fiscal reforms should eliminate or reduce the rate for PPE investment allowance to mitigate its misuse in lowering tax obligations. The study contributes to the literature by integrating governance and financial decision variables within a unified panel econometric framework and offers policy-relevant insights for tax regulation.

Keywords: Audit Quality, Corporate Investments Structure, Corporate Tax Avoidance.



1. INTRODUCTION

Corporate tax avoidance has become a major concern for policymakers and regulators, particularly in developing economies where revenue mobilization is critical for economic development. The phenomenon of tax avoidance is more prevalent among companies in Nigeria. Financial service firms, due to their complex financial structures and regulatory environment, present unique opportunities for tax planning and avoidance. Generally, firms consider taxes paid as an extra charge, significantly reducing the cash flow available. Hence, firms adopt diverse tax aggressiveness techniques to reduce their tax liability (Soliman & El-Sayed, 2023). The concern surrounding tax avoidance has significantly engaged tax administrators, practitioners, policymakers, and academic researchers in the global economy since the introduction of tax legislation. The practices create opportunities for managers to continue opportunistic behavior and hide bad news and mislead investors, which increases financial flexibility (Donkor et al., 2022). In addition, management considers the practices of tax avoidance as part of tax planning to affect economic activities and reduce the expected tax obligations, which affects the size, timing and degree of volatility of the firm’s cash flows (Lestari & Nedya, 2019).

Businesses exploit the deficiencies in tax laws to minimise their tax liabilities. Businesses commonly achieve this by decreasing their profit margin and reducing tax liability (Cao et al., 2021). Tax avoidance has been identified as one of the factors hindering economic growth in Nigeria (Nnenna, 2016) and presents a significant challenge for nations globally. Tax avoidance, while not violating applicable laws and regulations, is deemed immoral and unethical (Bae, 2017). Organizations perceived taxation as a mechanism that reduces their income without providing equivalent immediate compensation. Tax avoidance is a strategically organising a company's operational and financial activities and ownership structure to minimise tax liabilities (Gallemore & Labro, 2015). For example, the company's fixed assets constitute significant capital intensity, necessitating depreciation and capital allowances, which ultimately reduce corporate tax liability. Consequently, enterprises with large non-current assets will incur lower tax liabilities than those with fewer non-current assets (Heitzman & Ogneva, 2016; Bailling & Rul, 2018). Similarly, other strategies of corporate tax avoidance are tax savings, late payment of taxes, optimizing the permitted tax credit, avoiding tax audits by avoiding overpaying taxes, and avoiding violations of tax regulations (Sonia & Suparmun, 2019). However, corporate tax practices have negative effects such as an excessive increase in financial flexibility, and thus affecting the firm's reputation and the cost of debt. (Bailing & Rul, 2018).

Existing evidence on audit quality and tax avoidance is inconclusive. While strong governance is expected to reduce opportunistic behavior (Sikka, 2015), empirical findings suggest that audit mechanisms may be ineffective in weak institutional environments (Rizqia & Lastiati, 2021; Purba, 2018). At the same time, firms actively engage in tax planning through investment strategies that influence taxable income. Investment decisions, particularly in financial and non-financial assets, influence taxable income and, consequently, tax avoidance behavior. This study therefore, seeks to assess the impact of audit quality and corporate investments on corporate tax avoidance in Nigeria. Specifically, examines the impact of audit size, audit tenure, investment in short-term debt securities, and investment in property, plant, and equipment on tax avoidance by financial services firms in Nigeria. 

This study addresses two key gaps. First, it integrates audit quality and investment structure within a single empirical framework. Second, it provides evidence from Nigeria, an underexplored emerging economy characterized by institutional constraints and evolving regulatory systems. The study makes three contributions. First, it demonstrates that investment structure is a more powerful determinant of tax avoidance than audit quality. Second, it extends tax planning theory by linking asset allocation decisions to tax behavior. Third, it provides policy-relevant evidence from an emerging market context.


II. LITERATURE REVIEW AND THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

  • Conceptual Review

This section reviews relevant and related concepts on audit quality and tax avoidance.

    • Audit Quality and Tax Avoidance

Audit quality plays a significant role in determining and reflecting the transparency of a firm’s financial and non-financial reports (Moeinadin et al., 2013). The IAASB (2014) establishes that AQ comprises the essential elements that foster conducive environment to consistently perform high-quality audits. AQ is one of the determinants that reflect a high degree of assurance in the credibility and fairness of the firm's economic aspects. The professionalism of auditors is reflected in their ability to conduct audit assignments according to the laid-down professional standard.

The independent approach of auditors will likely impact the quality and effectiveness of activities in auditing and reporting the inaccuracies in the firm's financial statements (MCGuire et al., 2012). The findings of the research conducted by Shiba (2015) at the Tehran-based corporations demonstrated that audit tenure favours tax avoidance. Researchers contend that the extent of engagements between auditors and firms will enhance tax avoidance strategies. In a similar vein, Lestari and Nedya, (2019) reported that, increasing the length of audit firms by the Indonesian firms has given auditors the understanding and capability to develop effective and efficient audit procedures, due to the unique interaction between auditors and client, including helping businesses to further engage in tax avoidance strategies (Hu, 2018).  

Research by Jeong and Bae (2013) indicates that tax avoidance increases with the duration of the auditor's tenure, suggesting that prolonged relationships facilitate advantageous tax arrangements that reduce tax liabilities. Khajavi and Kiamehr (2015) reported similar findings, revealing that an auditor’s tenure positively impacts firms' intentions to evade tax. Bae (2017) finds a strong correlation between audit tenure and tax evasion. Frey (2018) found that the time of an audit firm's engagement with a company can positively influence the effective tax rate (ETR). Serafat and Barzegar (2015) identified a significant positive correlation between tax avoidance and audit tenure in Iran. Recent and previous studies have documented the effects of various audit quality variables on tax avoidance. Consequently, auditors' specialised knowledge would allow them to offer their customers high-quality tax services. This would make it possible for high-ranking audit firms to use tax planning tactics that are advantageous to their clients. This implies that businesses would pay less taxes if they employed quality auditors which would result in more tax avoidance (Chyz, et al. 2021; Monika & Noviari, 2021). 

Audit quality is widely viewed as a mechanism for constraining managerial opportunism. High-quality auditors are expected to enforce compliance and reduce aggressive tax practices. However, prolonged auditor–client relationships may impair independence, leading to ambiguous effects. Previous literature examined the direct association between audit quality and tax avoidance. However, the studies failed to investigate the indirect association of AQ and TA through audit size, tenure, investment in short-term debt securities, and investment in PPE. Thus, this research bridges this gap in the literature by investigating how audit size, audit tenure, investment in short-term debt securities, and investment in PPE affect corporate tax avoidance among financial services companies in Nigeria.

    • Literature Review and Hypotheses Development

This section reviews relevant and related literature in relation to the study variables and develop hypotheses that test and guide the study. 

      • Audit Size and Tax Avoidance

The size of the audit firm can influence tax avoidance practices. Big 4 audit firms are frequently expected to provide high audit services, which may likely reduce tax avoidance owing to heightened scrutiny and oversight. Some studies indicate that big audit firms may be more inclined to assist clients in implementing tax avoidance strategies, particularly for those with complex structures. Previous empirical studies (Pratama, 2017; Lee & Kao, 2018; Waluyo, 2019; Frey, 2018) have investigated the impact of audit size on tax avoidance and found that audit size exerts significant impact on tax avoidance.

Big Four auditors have established a strong reputation among the public due to the high credibility of their financial reports. Fadhilah, (2014) examines the relationship between audit firm size and tax avoidance and found that audit firm size exerts a negative impact on tax avoidance. This is supported by Suardana's (2014) findings, which reports that audit firm’s size negatively affects tax avoidance. In light of these various outcomes, this study hypothesizes that: 

H01: Audit size is negatively associated with tax avoidance of listed financial service firms in Nigeria.

      • Audit Tenure and Tax Avoidance

The duration of the relationship between an auditor and a client may impact the company's tax avoidance strategies. Research indicates that extended audit tenures may correlate with heightened tax avoidance, likely resulting from diminished auditors’ independence and an increased probability of auditors neglecting aggressive tax planning strategies. Some research suggests that audit tenure may not significantly influence corporate tax avoidance. On the other hand, a significant number of studies indicate that audit tenure has an impact on corporate tax avoidance in Nigeria. Jeong and Bae (2013) conducted an analysis of 2,588 firm-year observations of KOSPI spanning from 2001 to 2010. The outcomes of the research report a positive correlation between corporate tax avoidance and audit firms’ tenure. This indicates that long auditor relationships may enable tax strategies that reduce tax liabilities, which might otherwise increase with longer tenure.


Bae (2017) concluded that audit firms’ tenure positively correlates with tax avoidance. Similarly, the research findings in the Iranian market aligned with previous studies, for example, Frey (2018) found a significant positive relationship between the duration of the same audit firm's engagement and corporate tax avoidance. Serafat and Barzegar (2015) also found a significant positive relationship between tax avoidance and audit firms’ tenure within the Iranian context. Khajavi and Kiamehr (2015) conclude that audit tenure positively affects corporate tax avoidance. 

In view of the above conclusions, auditors are expected to gain a deeper understanding of the client's activities and to accumulate more experience over time (Salehi, Tarighi & Rezanezhad, 2018). A prolonged auditor–client working relationship enhances the quality of audit consultancy services, leading to a reduction in tax expenses. The economic situation in Iran suggests that an increase in the tenure of audit firms is likely to correlate with a rise in corporate tax avoidance. Consequently, the subsequent hypothesis of this study is articulated as follows:

H02: Audit tenure does not significantly influence firm's ability to avoid corporate taxes in the Nigerian financial service sector.

      • Corporate Investment and Tax Avoidance 

Corporate investment refers to actions undertaken by firms to allocate specific funds to maintain or enhance value, or generate a positive return for the organisation (Widuri, Naomi, Bumulo & Wijaya, 2020). Incorporating investment planning into decision models allows for deriving anticipated benefits from the investment. To prevent inadequate or excessive investment, business management must closely monitor strategy and develop an optimal investment plan. Studies indicate that corporate investment and tax planning typically result in corporate tax avoidance. Ding (2019) examines the impact of corporate tax avoidance on investment efficiency through the lenses of knowledge asymmetry and conflicts of interest. The study's findings indicate a positive correlation, suggesting that tax avoidance enhances investment efficiency. Widuri et al. (2020) analysed publicly traded non-financial firms on the Indonesia Stock Exchange. The analysis indicates a positive correlation between tax avoidance and ineffective investment. The findings indicate that management decisions have an impact on tax avoidance.

Park, Park, Sun, and Woo (2016) examine the extent to which multinational corporations (MNCs) utilise subsidiaries for tax avoidance in Korea. The study's outcomes manifest in two forms. MNCs that expand their global diversification by establishing overseas subsidiaries generally demonstrate a greater tendency to evade tax obligations. The findings demonstrate a correlation between globally diversified multinational corporations and corporate tax avoidance. This correlation was established owing to the companies' active use of tax planning (investment tax credits, tax reductions) relevant to the numerous nations where they have grown their operations. However, compared to businesses without foreign subsidiaries, the findings revealed that organizations intentionally avoided tax by engaging in international transfer pricing practices. The parent company's tendency to evade taxation rose with corresponding modifications in sales prices and purchase values via actual transactions. This study is essential as it illustrates how businesses can utilise foreign direct investment and international transfer pricing methods to reduce their tax liabilities. This elucidates the recent trend of multinational corporations enhancing their initiatives by establishing additional local manufacturing facilities.

Achyar and Yulianti (2021) and Cen, Maydew, Zhang, and Zuo (2017) investigate the extent to which firms with strong supplier-customer relationships identify and utilise strategies for supply-chain tax avoidance. The research found that significant customers and suppliers engage in tax avoidance more frequently than other enterprises, corroborating our assumption. The findings indicated that suppliers and customers primarily engaged in profit-shifting tax strategies involving substantial investments in subsidiaries located in tax haven jurisdictions. Widuri et al. (2020) and Khurana, Moser, and Raman (2018) examine the influence of management skills on the relationship between investment efficiency and corporate tax avoidance. The results indicate a positive correlation between tax avoidance and investment efficiency. The relationship between tax avoidance and investment inefficiency is moderated by managerial ability.

Asiri et al. (2020) and Ding (2019) examine the effects of corporate tax avoidance on investment efficiency, focusing on information asymmetry and agency conflict. The research findings indicate a positive relationship between tax avoidance and investment effectiveness. Hasan and Habib (2021) examined the correlation between corporate tax avoidance and asset redeploy ability in the United States in 2021. The research found that asset redistribution directly and indirectly reduces tax avoidance by alleviating financing constraints. Ngelo et al. (2022) investigated Indonesian firms' investment efficiency in tax avoidance, revealing a positive relationship between tax avoidance and investment efficiency. The research found that the investment efficiency of tax avoidance is significant in firms prone to under- and over-investment. Investment decisions influence tax outcomes through depreciation, capital allowances, and financial income structures. Financial investments provide flexibility for tax planning, while PPE investments impose structured tax treatments. Therefore, the study hypotheses that:

H03: Investment in short-term debt securities does not significantly affect corporate tax avoidance among the Nigerian Financial service firms.

H04: Investment in PPE does not significantly influence corporate tax avoidance among financial firms in Nigeria.

    • Theoretical Framework

This section reviews relevant and related theory on corporate tax avoidance. Hoffman's tax planning theory was used to underpin this study. 

      • Hoffman Tax Planning Theory

Walter Hoffman's tax planning theory (1961) emphasises strategies that individuals and businesses can employ to minimise tax liabilities while adhering to legal frameworks. Hoffman's work emphasises the necessity of comprehending tax laws, regulations, and tax planning opportunities to attain financial efficiency and compliance. The theory asserts that inherent loopholes in the legal system are inevitable and allow taxpayers to gain advantages. The theory emphasises that tax planning facilitates savings by minimising taxable income (Emmanuel & Ifeoma, 2023). A fundamental aspect of Hoffman's theory is income recognition and the timing of deductions. Strategic planning regarding the timing of income recognition and deduction claims allows taxpayers to leverage variations in tax rates over time, thereby minimising their overall tax liability. Hoffman’s theory emphasises the significance of income shifting, which entails reallocating income to taxpayers situated in lower tax brackets or jurisdictions with reduced tax rates. Methods to achieve this include family income splitting, establishing trusts, or reallocating income to corporations or subsidiaries located in lower-tax jurisdictions. This study extends these frameworks by introducing investment structure as the primary channel through which tax planning is operationalized, particularly in environments where governance mechanisms are weak.




III. METHODOLOGY

This research concentrated solely on the quantitative research paradigm, which is inherently associated with positivism (Creswell, 2012). The study employs a quantitative research design, which effectively facilitates the validation or refutation of a hypothesis. This is accomplished by determining the influence of the explanatory (independent) variables on the explained (dependent) variable. The study population comprises all 53 financial service companies listed on the Nigerian Exchange Group as of December 31, 2021. The selection of financial service firms is justified by their significant contribution to the Nigerian economy, accounting for approximately 70% of market capitalisation (NSE, 2018). However, thirteen (13) inactive firms were removed from the population. The elimination yielded a sample size of forty (40) firms, representing approximately 75.5% of the population. The data were obtained from the published financial statements of the selected firms. The actions led to the collection of balanced panel data, consisting of 40 firms over a seven-year period (2015 to 2021), yielding a total of 280 observations. The choice of 2015 as the base year is due to its alignment with the adoption of the electronic tax system in Nigeria (Ofurum et al., 2018). The study focuses exclusively on the electronic taxation period to avoid conflating compliance rates with those of manual tax administration. 

Multiple regression model was employed to analyse the data. This involves estimating the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), Heteroscedasticity, Lagrange Multiplier, and Hausman Specification tests. These tests follow the execution of normality and multicollinearity assessments, including the pre-estimation test. The results of these tests inform the research direction regarding the selection of the OLS model or the Generalised Least Squares (GLS), incorporating Fixed and Random Effect (FE and RE) models.

    • Inferential Analysis

The section summarises the pooled results of the OLS Model, the Fixed Effects Model, and the Random Effects Model. The study tested the stationarity and made the necessary adjustments so that the variables fit the models with the same order of integration. To start the empirical analysis, the calculation commenced with estimating a Pool OLS model with constant intercept and slope coefficients across firms and time, assuming neither a significant cross-sectional nor a significant temporal effect.  

The value of R-Squared is estimated at 0.3109, suggesting that the pool OLS model accounts for approximately 31% of the factors that impact corporate tax avoidance. However, due to the inability of pool OLS to account for within effects (firm’s effects) and omitted variable bias, it is necessary to test the appropriateness of the model. Expectedly, the OLS model is statistically significant at 1%. Although this model fits the data, as earlier stated, the dataset contains fewer cross-sections and more time observations, making it necessary to check for the appropriateness of the Pool OLS model over FE and RE models.  Certainly, if ordinary OLS methods are applied to non-stationary data, the outcome may likely be misleading (Newbold & Granger, 1974). 

The Hausman specification test was computed to determine which one of the two models (Panel FE or RE) is appropriate. Although Clark, Richardson and Vasvari (2011) argue that the Hausman specification test may be inappropriate in choosing between FE and RE models if the results of the test are not significant, the result of the Hausman specification test is significant (p<0.05). Based on the Hausman test, the FE model is more reliable as the P-value is significant at the 1% level.

The study used graphical and numerical methods to determine the homoscedasticity of residuals in the data. Using the graphical method, the study plotted a graph of the residuals (r) values versus the predicted value using the “rvfplot” command after OLS regression. The decision rule is that if the data point pattern is slightly tighter in the direction of the right end, it indicates that the residuals are heteroscedastic. From Figure 4.1, the data shape point is getting wider toward the right end, showing the absence of heteroscedasticity of residuals.

This result is supported by the numerical methods using the white and Breusch-pagan tests. The results in Table 4.5 show that the p-value is 0.0000 for both the white and Breusch-pagan tests. These large p-values provide adequate evidence not to reject the null hypothesis that the variance is homogeneous, meaning the residual is homoscedastic. The presence of autocorrelation in panel data was further tested. Ignoring serial correlation when it is present results in inconsistent and inefficient estimates of the regression coefficients and biased standard errors.  The analysis tests the null of zero first-order serial correlation, the presence of higher-order serial correlation, and the presence of individual effects. The decision rule is that an insignificant probability value indicates the absence of autocorrelation. The statistical result shows the calculated value as F=1.161 and an insignificant probability value of 0.3304. Therefore, the analysis rejects the null of no first-order serial correlation. 4.6 Discussions of Findings

The result established that Audit size impacts book tax difference positively, signifying that the more financial service firms engage professional accountants as external auditors, the higher their book tax differences. This high book tax difference implies an increase in tax avoidance. However, the p-value of audit size in Table 4.5 is 0.411, which is higher than 0.05, indicating an insignificant consequence on tax avoidance. The negative effect of audit size on tax avoidance could be attributed to the specialisation of professional accounting firms engaged by financial firms. Furthermore, while most sample firms do not engage the services of professional accountants, a few patronise them. This will decrease the transparency of the quality of financial reporting, thereby paving the way for firms to engage in tax avoidance. These tax avoidance activities will cause difficult transactions, negatively affect the firms' information environment, and decrease transparency in financial reporting.












This finding supported the first null hypothesis, which states that audit size does not play a vigorous role in affecting tax avoidance of the financial service firms in Nigeria. This finding is in line with the study of Frey (2018), Purba (2018), Amalia & Ferdiansyah (2019), Monika & Noviari (2021), Wulandari & Sudarma (2021), Nurjanah & Aligarh (2021) and Gunawan, Christy, Tin and Jonathan (2021) which found that audit quality by audit size have no significant effect on tax avoidance. However, the finding contradicts the studies of Pratama (2017), Lelekaei and Nikkar (2017), Suyono (2018), Marselawati, Titisari, and Masitoh (2018), Lestari & Nedya (2019), Waluyo (2019), Tarmidi, Sari, and Sormin (2020), Riguen, Salhi, and Jarboui (2021) and Abbas Akbar & Ahmed (2022) documenting that audit size has a significant effect on tax avoidance.

The study further established that audit tenure has an insignificant negative effect on book tax difference. This indicates that the longer financial firms engage the professional accounting firms, the lower their book tax difference. This low book tax difference implies a decrease in tax avoidance. Audit tenure negatively affects tax avoidance, proving that it encourages tax avoidance activities of the sampled financial firms. It is clear from the results that the quality of the audit consulting service will enhance whenever the client-auditor working relationship strengthens, thereby pulling down tax expenses. In addition, it is undeniable that many audit firms are compelled to forgo investing in audit technologies and senior staff with experience due to their low-income levels. Managers' capacity to rent-extract tax savings to increase personal wealth is not constrained. Results of this study imply that obligatory auditor rotation may not impair the external auditor's monitoring function in the capital market.

This finding is contrary to the studies of Lestari & Nedya (2019), Brooks & Sun (2020), Salehi, Tarighi and Shahri (2020), Chyz, Gal-Or, Naiker, and Sharma (2021), which found that audit quality by audit tenure has a significant positive consequence on tax avoidance, and the studies of Tarmidi, Sari, and Sormin (2020), Lelekaei and Nikkar (2017), documenting that audit tenure has a significant negative effect on tax avoidance. The study also contradicts the results of Kuncoro and Surjandari (2023), documenting that audit tenure affects tax avoidance.

The result also established that the amount of investment in short-term debt securities positively affects book tax difference, indicating that the more financial service firms invest in short-term debt securities, the higher their book tax difference. This high book tax difference implies an increase in tax avoidance. The negativity of the amount invested in short-term debt securities by financial service firms in Nigeria may not be unconnected to the fact that most of the sampled firms largely invest in long-term debt securities other than short-term debt securities that mature within one or two years. These results provide adequate evidence for rejecting the third null hypothesis, indicating that the amount of investment in short-term debt securities plays a vital role in affecting the tax avoidance of the financial service firms in Nigeria. This finding agrees with Hoffman's tax planning theory (Hoffman, 1961), which assumes that tax planning events are required to the point that they decrease taxable income significantly, without forfeiting accounting income. The theory assumes that the tax liabilities of firms depend on taxable profits instead of accounting profits. The impression is therefore to strengthen activities that decrease taxable profits for more accounting profit.

The analysis mirrors that the amount of PPE investment significantly negatively affects the book tax difference. This points out that the more investment in PPE, the higher their book tax differences. This high book tax difference implies an increase in tax avoidance. It therefore follows that the amount of investment in PPE plays a vital role in affecting tax avoidance in Nigeria. This finding contradicts the outcomes of Hoffman's tax planning theory (Hoffman, 1961), which assumes that the tax liabilities of firms depend on taxable profits instead of accounting profits. The impression is therefore to strengthen activities that decrease taxable profits for more accounting profit. The theory further assumes that Tax planning events are required to the point that they decrease taxable income significantly, without forfeiting accounting income.

  • CONCLUSION

The study concludes that audit size and audit tenure do not affect tax avoidance for financial services firms in Nigeria. This signifies that external auditor do not lead to an increase or decrease in taxable income of the sampled firms under study. Similarly, the investment amount in short-term debt securities discourages tax avoidance practices in the sampled companies. The result shapes companies’ confidence about the quantum of investment in short-term debt securities and their contributions towards reducing tax avoidance activities of financial service firms in Nigeria.  At the same time, the Amount of investment in PPE encourages tax avoidance practices in the sampled companies.

  • RECOMMENDATIONS

The study offered the following recommendations. Investors and financial service providers should exercise caution when dealing with external audit firms. Policy makers should ensure that the activities of Big Four audit firms, especially concerning DMBs in Nigeria, are probed to ensure that relevant auditing standards are strictly adhered to. The audit regulatory body in Nigeria should modify the auditing standards to incorporate laws regulating the maximum number of years that external auditors – professional firms or otherwise – are engaged by their clients to minimise familiarity and possible fraud in financial statements. Through the Nigerian lawmakers, the federal government of Nigeria should revisit the policy on tax-exempt items concerning investment in short-term debt securities. This is because financial service firms take the opportunity to engage in corporate tax planning activities to minimise their tax liabilities.

  • IMPLICATIONS OF FINDINGS 

The outcomes of this study have theoretical, methodological, practical, and policy implications. The results empirically support the agency viewpoint of corporate tax avoidance in relation to theory. The agency framework is typically applied to business results as a directly preservative function that simulates management incentives, oversight, bonding costs, and residual loss as functions that affect firm outcomes. In the normal interpretation of the model, interactions between the mechanisms or between many other potentially significant qualities are not taken into account. Practically, the findings suggest that the extent of audit firm engagement may not necessarily result in tax avoidance, as it is expected that audit firms may have familiarised themselves with the management, which will lead them to behave in the company's interests. It is also expected that the optimal interest in this situation would be more tax avoidance, since it would result in higher shareholder profits.
